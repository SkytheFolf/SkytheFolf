# Verizon Pixels stay sealed, so buy unlocked

As of October 2026, there is no official, unofficial, or paid method that unlocks the bootloader of a genuine Verizon-SKU Google Pixel, from the Pixel 3 through the Pixel 10. Without an unlocked bootloader you cannot install AOSP builds or custom ROMs such as GrapheneOS, CalyxOS, or LineageOS. The block comes from a carrier ID written to the phone's persist partition at the factory plus an online "sold carrier-locked?" check. It is separate from Verizon's SIM (network) unlock, so paying off the phone or waiting out Verizon's unlock period does not change it. Verizon's 2026 policy changes made SIM unlocking slower: the FCC waived its 60-day auto-unlock rule in January 2026, and prepaid phones now wait 365 days. Those changes have no effect on the bootloader, which was never unlockable under the old rules either. The only exploits that ever worked were for the 2016 Pixel and the 2017 Pixel 2 (non-XL), and all of them were patched or tied to those specific models. Running custom ROMs is legal under the DMCA §1201 jailbreaking exemption, but nothing in the law requires Verizon or Google to make the bootloader unlockable. Meanwhile AOSP is still open source, but Google has made it harder for Pixel ROMs to keep up: private development, twice-yearly source drops, and no Pixel device trees since Android 16. For a Verizon Pixel owner who wants a custom ROM, the realistic path is to sell or trade in the Verizon unit and buy a Google Store or retail-unlocked Pixel that is confirmed bootloader-unlockable. GrapheneOS on a Pixel 8–10 is the strongest option in 2026.

## AOSP remains open source, but Google stopped publishing Pixel support in 2025

The Android Open Source Project (AOSP) is the openly licensed code base of Android. Custom ROMs are distributions built from it: they take AOSP, add device-specific drivers and firmware ("vendor" blobs and device trees), and change, harden, or de-Google the result. Pixels have long been the default hardware for ROMs because Google published reference device support for them and lets owners unlock the bootloader. Both of those assumptions weakened between 2025 and 2026.

On **March 26, 2025**, Google moved all Android OS development to its internal branch. Public source is now published only after each release, and the old public `aosp-main` branch is read-only. Developers are directed to `android-latest-release` instead ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-streamlining-android-development-3538503/); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/?p=666914)). In June 2025, the **Android 16 AOSP release shipped without Pixel device trees or driver binaries**. Google made the virtual device "Cuttlefish" the reference target instead. Android VP Seang Chau insisted "AOSP is NOT going away" ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/06/12/android-open-source-project-pixel-change/); [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/google-not-killing-aosp-3566882)). LineageOS's Nolen Johnson said developers would have to "blindly guess and reverse engineer" changes. CalyxOS said AOSP 16 "cannot currently be built or run on any recent Pixel device easily just using official source" ([CalyxOS](https://calyxos.org/news/2025/06/11/android-16-plans/)). Then, from **2026, Google publishes source only twice a year**: in Q2 for the major release and Q4 for the minor one. Pixels still get four releases a year, and security patches flow through a separate branch ([Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/aosp-source-code-schedule-3630018/); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/google-set-to-reduce-the-android-source-code-releases-to-just-twice-a-year)).

The practical result is a lag. Android 16 QPR1 reached Pixels on September 3, 2025, but reached AOSP only on November 11, 2025. Android 17 QPR1 reached Pixels on September 15, 2026, and its source is not expected in AOSP until QPR2 in December 2026. GrapheneOS says this leaves it reverse-engineering security fixes in the meantime ([GrapheneOS forum](https://discuss.grapheneos.org/d/25099-pixel-10-support-for-grapheneos-is-now-available/233); [next.ink](https://next.ink/256965/grapheneos-deplore-lattitude-de-google-sur-les-correctifs-de-securite-dandroid/)). CalyxOS likewise says it cannot ship updated bootloader, modem, and vendor firmware on time ([CalyxOS](https://calyxos.org/news/2026/08/07/august-security-update/)). Building "pure AOSP" for a recent Pixel is therefore no longer a supported path. Google's build docs still require a 64-bit x86 Linux machine with **at least 64 GB of RAM and 400 GB of free disk** ([source.android.com](https://source.android.com/setup/start)), but most owners install a ROM project's signed images instead. The projects with their own vendor-extraction tooling were affected least. GrapheneOS overhauled its adevtool "to fully replace the need for Android device trees" ([Developer-Tech](https://www.developer-tech.com/news/android-open-future-questioned-google-withholds-pixel-code/)).

| GrapheneOS | Pixel 6 and later, including the full Pixel 10 line (production since Jan 10, 2026) and the 10a | Yes, custom AVB key | Android 17 ported immediately after the June 16, 2026 release; Pixel 11 support "not determined yet" ([GrapheneOS forum](https://discuss.grapheneos.org/d/25099-pixel-10-support-for-grapheneos-is-now-available); [anoni.net](https://anoni.net/docs/en/blog/2026/07/2026-grapheneos-android-17/); [AndroidHeadlines](https://androidheadlines.com/2025/10/grapheneos-may-break-pixel-exclusivity-with-a-new-phone-in-2026.html)) |

All three require one thing first: a bootloader that accepts `fastboot flashing unlock`. That requirement is where Verizon Pixels fail.

## A factory carrier ID blocks the bootloader, and SIM unlocking does not remove it

On a locked Pixel, Android Verified Boot only boots images signed by Google or by a user-enrolled custom key. Installing anything else requires enabling "OEM unlocking" in Developer options and then running `fastboot flashing unlock`, which wipes the device. If the setting stays disabled, AOSP's own documentation says the device "may be SIM-locked by the carrier" and the bootloader cannot be unlocked ([source.android.com](https://source.android.com/source/running)).

Verizon Pixels run the same firmware as unlocked Pixels. GrapheneOS explains the difference: carrier variants have "a non-zero carrier id flashed onto the persist partition in the factory," which "activates carrier-specific configuration in the stock OS including disabling carrier and bootloader unlocking" ([GrapheneOS forum](https://discuss.grapheneos.org/d/10362-oem-possibly-carrier-locked-phone)). On SKUs that can be sold carrier-locked, enabling OEM unlocking also "requires internet access so that the stock OS can check if the device was sold as locked by a carrier" ([GrapheneOS forum](https://discuss.grapheneos.org/d/21860-oem-unlocking-do-you-need-an-internet-connection)). CalyxOS puts the result bluntly: **"Verizon Pixels prohibit their users from unlocking bootloader, thus, you cannot install any custom Android OS on it"** ([CalyxOS](https://calyxos.org/install/verizon/)). Verizon representatives agree: "We do not unlock the bootloader on our devices." Another Verizon agent said the carrier has no access to the option at all ([Verizon Community](https://community.verizon.com/t5/Google-Pixel/Pixel-OEM-Unlocking-Support/td-p/1153573/page/2); [Verizon Community](https://community.verizon.com/t5/Google-Pixel/Pixel-OEM-Unlocking-Support/td-p/1153573/page/9)).

Verizon's SIM unlock is a separate mechanism, and it got more restrictive in 2026. On **January 12, 2026**, the FCC waived the 60-day automatic-unlock obligation attached to Verizon's 700 MHz C-block license. Verizon now only has to follow the voluntary CTIA code, and the waiver lasts until the FCC adopts an industry-wide framework ([FCC DOC-417932A1](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-417932A1.pdf); [Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/fcc-grants-verizons-request-for-unlocking-waiver)). Verizon cited fraud, including about 785,000 devices lost to fraud in 2023 ([Droid-Life](https://www.droid-life.com/2026/01/13/verizon-convinces-fcc-to-kill-its-60-day-phone-unlocking-rule-and-thats-bad/)). Under the new policy, **prepaid phones unlock after 365 days of paid, active service**, postpaid phones unlock when fully paid off, and some payment methods add a 35-day delay. Phones bought before late January 2026 keep the 60-day rule. Reported effective dates vary between January 20, January 27, and February 18, 2026 ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/01/20/verizon-device-unlock-policy-365-day/); [SamMobile](https://www.sammobile.com/news/samsung-phones-on-verizon-now-harder-to-unlock/); [Verizon](https://www.verizon.com/about/consumer-safety/device-unlocking-policy)).

None of this affects the bootloader. A Google Pixel Community answer notes that Verizon-associated Pixels, "even if carrier unlocked," still have bootloaders that cannot be unlocked ([Google Pixel Help](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/thread/318109388/oem-unlock-disabled-pixel-3xl?hl=en)). One Verizon customer was told OEM unlocking would appear 60 days after activation, and it never did ([Verizon Community](https://community.verizon.com/discussion/1829240/bootloader-unlock-request-pixel-7)). Reports across Pixel 6a, 7, 8, and 9-era threads say the same thing. One GrapheneOS user wrote that the toggle is "grayed out on all Verizon devices, even if you paid full price." Another wrote that if a device won't allow OEM unlocking, "it's fairly likely that it's permanent and no sequence of commands will change that" ([GrapheneOS forum](https://discuss.grapheneos.org/d/15210-verizon-and-gos); [GrapheneOS forum](https://discuss.grapheneos.org/d/11018-bought-carrier-locked-pixel-7-and-cannot-unlock-oem/13); [Lemmy](https://lemmy.ndlug.org/post/1524967/3081223)). The handful of counter-reports (a toggle that appeared after a factory reset, or a used Verizon 7a found already unlocked) are unverified. They most plausibly involve non-Verizon-SKU phones sold through Verizon-adjacent channels, such as a US Mobile Pixel 9 that unlocked after a reset on Wi-Fi ([GrapheneOS forum](https://discuss.grapheneos.org/d/23403-us-mobile-offering-250-pixel-9-will-it-work-with-grapheneos); [GrapheneOS forum](https://discuss.grapheneos.org/d/24394-got-a-used-pixel-7a-from-verizon-but-the-bootloader-seems-to-be-unlocked)). No reports specific to Verizon Pixel 10 or Pixel 11 units turned up, so the claim that they are permanently locked rests on the unchanged mechanism, not on direct evidence.

## Every known exploit ended with the Pixel 2

The history is short. Each method worked on one generation and stopped working soon after.

| Device | Method | Date | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pixel / Pixel XL | dePixel8 (beaups and jcase, Sunshine team) forced OEM unlock on Verizon and EE units | Oct 2016 | Patched; Verizon build NMF26O believed to block it ([Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/2016/10/28/unlock-bootloader-pixel-phones-verizon-ee-depixel8-beaups-jcase-hurry/); [XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/verizon-pixelpixel-xl-bootloader-unlock-has-been-released/)) |

| Pixel 2 (HTC, non-XL) | Plain fastboot `flashing lock_critical` / `unlock_critical` quirk | Dec 2017 | Model-specific; current status on final firmware is conflicting ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/verizon-pixel-2-unlocked-bootloader-826447); [Hovatek](https://www.hovatek.com/forum/archive/index.php/thread-45145.html)) |
| Pixel 2 XL through Pixel 10 | None public | — | No known exploit ([Hovatek](https://www.hovatek.com/forum/archive/index.php/thread-45145.html); [providence.root.sx](https://providence.root.sx/post/224844)) |

About eight years have passed without a public bypass for any generation after the Pixel 2. Anti-rollback also matters here. Since Android 13, Pixel 6-series bootloaders refuse older builds ("If this is FLASHED you CANNOT go back") ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/android-13-downgrade-protection-pixel-6-series/)), so even a hypothetical exploit tied to old firmware could not be reached by downgrading. Any **paid "Verizon Pixel bootloader unlock" service should therefore be presumed a scam or a relabeled SIM unlock**. The legitimate historical tools were free ([Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/2016/10/28/unlock-bootloader-pixel-phones-verizon-ee-depixel8-beaups-jcase-hurry/)), Verizon's own staff cannot toggle the setting, and Verizon offers SIM unlocks free under its policy. A service that asks for remote ADB access, your Google credentials, or untraceable payment puts your data and the phone itself at risk. This research found no named service to evaluate.

## Legal to modify, but no law requires Verizon or Google to allow it

The law does not stand in the way. The obstacle is technical. The Copyright Office's DMCA §1201 exemption for jailbreaking smartphones to run lawfully obtained software, including operating systems "from any source" in EFF's description, was **renewed in October 2024 and runs through about October 2027** ([EFF](https://www.eff.org/press/releases/eff-wins-renewal-smartphone-jailbreaking-rights-plus-new-legal-protections-video); [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/is-jailbreaking-your-phone-illegal/)). Separately, the **Unlocking Consumer Choice and Wireless Competition Act (Public Law 113-144, signed August 1, 2014)** protects owners who network-unlock a handset, provided they have met their contract obligations ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/113/plaws/publ144/PLAW-113publ144.htm); [Obama White House](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/node/294151)). Both laws protect users from liability for circumvention. Neither obliges a carrier or manufacturer to provide an unlock path. "Is it legal?" and "Is it possible?" are separate questions on a Verizon Pixel, and the second answer is no. Users who exploit their own devices are better covered than people who build or sell circumvention tools, because the exemptions do not cover §1201's anti-trafficking provisions. Financed Verizon phones carry an additional contractual constraint. This research could not retrieve the specific device-modification clauses in Verizon's Customer Agreement.

## Unlocking costs app compatibility even on phones that allow it

Owners should know these costs before switching phones. Unlocking always wipes the device ([developer.android.com](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/download)). On Android 13 and later, Play Integrity's default **MEETS_DEVICE_INTEGRITY verdict requires hardware-backed proof of a locked bootloader and a certified OS**. Since May 2025 this stricter check is the default ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/verdicts); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-play-integrity-hardware-attestation-3561592/)). Spoofing modules now claim only BASIC integrity ([PlayIntegrityFix README](https://github.com/KillaKev/PlayIntegrityFix_KOWX712)). The consequences are that Google Wallet tap-to-pay fails on every custom ROM, and some banking, streaming, and enterprise apps refuse to run. GrapheneOS reduces the damage by relocking the bootloader with its own AVB key, which keeps verified boot and rollback protection, and by offering apps a hardware-attestation path. Most banking apps work on it, but Wallet does not, and a 2026 account says many banks still refuse it ([GrapheneOS forum](https://discuss.grapheneos.org/d/8330-app-compatibility-with-grapheneos); [urandom.io](https://urandom.io/blog/2026-02-17-grapheneos-the-pixel-paradox)). LineageOS leaves the bootloader unlocked, which gives up protection against anyone with physical access who flashes modified images ([iodé community](https://community.iode.tech/t/any-way-to-avoid-your-phone-has-loaded-a-different-operating-system/7315)). Widevine downgrades to L3 (no HD Netflix) are documented on other brands. Data for current Pixels is thin, and 2026 guides still list an unlocked bootloader as a cause ([Android Magazine](https://androidmagazine.eu/en/2026/02/11/proc-netflix-a-dalsi-videa-na-androidu-nejsou-v-hd-3-tipy-jak-to-rychle-napravit/)). Google's warranty position is undocumented. Past reports say Google repairs modified Pixels but requires stock software for exchanges, so relocking to stock before an RMA is the safe habit ([Ubergizmo](https://www.ubergizmo.com/2017/10/pixel-2-rooting-not-void-warranty)). One caution applies to carrier-detected devices: if you relock and turn OEM unlocking off, you may never be able to turn it back on ([GrapheneOS forum](https://discuss.grapheneos.org/d/17182-bought-carrier-network-unlocked-device-not-oem-unlocked)).

## The practical path: verify the phone, then switch to an unlocked Pixel

Start by confirming what you have. Boot stock Android, connect to Wi-Fi, and open Developer options. If OEM unlocking can be toggled, the phone is not Verizon-SKU-locked. A greyed-out toggle while offline proves nothing ([CalyxOS](https://calyxos.org/install/verizon/)). A stock build or update list that names Verizon, or a firmware string like "ms-android-verizon", marks a carrier unit ([GrapheneOS forum](https://discuss.grapheneos.org/d/12844-purchased-unlocked-pixel-8a-but-the-oem-unlocking-option-is-disabled/16); [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/Google-apparently-sent-Verizon-locked-Pixel-3-to-some-who-ordered-the-unlocked-model_id111317)). Verizon's BYOD IMEI checker can label a phone "Non-VZW" before you buy it ([GrapheneOS forum](https://discuss.grapheneos.org/d/20348-oem-unlock-grayed-pixel-7)). Model numbers are not a reliable signal, because carrier and unlocked SKUs share them.

If the phone is a true Verizon unit, there are three paths. The first is to keep it stock and make it cleaner: Shizuku plus Canta performs a per-user `pm uninstall --user 0` without a PC or root, and a factory reset restores everything ([Kevin Boone](https://kevinboone.me/canta.html)). DSU can only boot GSIs "signed by Google or your OEMs", so it cannot run GrapheneOS or LineageOS on a locked phone ([developer.android.com/dsu](https://developer.android.com/dsu)). The second path, and the real fix, is to **pay off any financing, then sell or trade in the Verizon Pixel and buy a Google Store or retail-unlocked one**. Pixels sold by Google come with unlockable bootloaders ([GSMArena](https://m.gsmarena.com/newscomm-21089.php)), and Google Store Pixels have long supported Verizon VoLTE and Wi-Fi calling ([PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/Pixel-Phones-bought-from-the-Google-Store-will-be-compatible-with-Verizon-HD-Voice-and-Wi-Fi-calling_id86506)). Some older unlocked models lacked Verizon mmWave ([Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/2020/09/30/here-are-the-5g-networks-your-pixel-5-or-pixel-4a-5g-can-use/)). Current band parity for the Pixel 9 and 10 was not verified. The third path applies to used purchases: insist on a listing marked "bootloader unlockable" with a return window ([Swappa](https://swappa.com/listing/view/LYAY14024)), and test the toggle yourself on Wi-Fi after a reset. Even Google-sold units have occasionally shipped with the option disabled ([GrapheneOS forum](https://discuss.grapheneos.org/d/12844-purchased-unlocked-pixel-8a-but-the-oem-unlocking-option-is-disabled/60)).

For the ROM itself, choose by device. On a **Pixel 8, 9, or 10, GrapheneOS is the clear choice**: it has production Pixel 10 support, the fastest Android 17 port, a relockable bootloader, and support windows that secondary sources put at 2032–2033 ([efani](https://www.efani.com/blog/grapheneos-supported-devices)). CalyxOS suits Pixel 9-and-earlier owners who want microG-style convenience and accept its lag. LineageOS is the option for Pixel 4- and 5-era hardware. Expect up to about three months between a stock Pixel QPR release and the matching ROM release.

## Conclusion

The Verizon bootloader lock has become more stable while everything around it has changed. Carrier unlock law, FCC rules, and AOSP publishing all shifted between 2025 and 2026, but the factory carrier ID has held for eight hardware generations without a public bypass. The 2026 changes made SIM unlocking slower. They also make it more likely that a used "unlocked" Verizon Pixel is still SIM-locked, and it was always going to be bootloader-locked. For buyers, "bootloader unlockable" now has to be verified separately from "carrier unlocked", and should never be assumed from it.

The longer-term risk has moved from the carrier to Google. By withholding Pixel device trees and quarterly source, Google has shifted the cost of running custom ROMs onto the few projects that can reverse-engineer vendor code. GrapheneOS's own statement that Pixel 11 support is "not determined yet", together with its work on non-Pixel OEM hardware, suggests that the default custom-ROM phone may stop being a Pixel within a few years. Anyone buying hardware today for long-term ROM use should pick an unlocked Pixel 8 through 10 that is already supported, rather than count on future generations.
